01 The Experience

More than a business class.

Business Lab combines business education with hands-on product development.

Students spend one week learning about business with a business teacher, then the following week working with a specialty instructor to develop the product they will eventually bring to market.

Over eight weeks, the pieces come together. In Week 9, students put their learning into action at a real student market, where each participant has their own booth and sells their product.

02 How the Lab Works

Learn it. Build it. Share it.

The program alternates between business instruction and hands-on product development so students can immediately apply what they're learning.

03 Choose a Product Pathway

What will they make?

Students choose a creative pathway for the hands-on portion of the program. The pathway gives them a starting point while leaving room for their own ideas.

For students who love designing, building, crafting, drawing, inventing, or figuring out how things work.

POSSIBILITIES

Art prints, stickers, jewelry, accessories, games, kits, crafts, textile projects, inventions and upcycled creations.

THE GOAL

Develop something they can proudly present as a finished product at the Student Market.

For students who love baking, experimenting with recipes, decorating treats, or imagining their own food creations.

POSSIBILITIES

Cookies, brownies, breads, cupcakes, decorated treats, recipe products, baking kits, mixes and other food-based ideas.

THE GOAL

Explore how a favorite recipe or food idea can become something customers would want to buy.

For students who are fascinated by plants, gardening, growing things, nature, or the environment around them.

POSSIBILITIES

Starter plants, herbs, seedlings, flowers, seed packets, garden kits, botanical art, nature journals and other growing projects.

THE GOAL

Develop something that connects their interest in nature with a useful, beautiful, or shareable product.

For students who love collecting, researching, hunting for interesting things, or becoming deeply knowledgeable about a subject.

POSSIBILITIES

Curated collections, themed boxes, collectibles, specialty finds, research-based products, guides and carefully selected items.

THE GOAL

Learn how knowledge, discovery, presentation and curation can become something others value.

WEEK 9

Then they open for business.

The Student Market is the culmination of the eight-week experience.

Each student gets their own booth and the opportunity to present their product to real customers.

It's a chance to practice everything they've been working on — from presentation and pricing to communication and selling.

05 Why Business Lab?

A program where learning has somewhere to go.

Students aren't simply completing worksheets about entrepreneurship. They are learning concepts, applying them, creating something, making decisions, and eventually putting their work in front of customers.

The structure also gives students opportunities to work both collaboratively and independently.

Most importantly, they get to experience the connection between an idea, the work behind it, and the people who ultimately interact with it.

06 Questions
Does my child need to already have a business idea? +

No. Business Lab is designed to help students explore ideas and develop a product throughout the program.

Does every student make the same type of product? +

No. Students choose from four product pathways — Maker, Baker, Nature, or Curator — and develop their own product within that pathway.

What happens at the end of the program? +

Week 9 is the Student Market. Each student has their own booth where they present and sell their product.

Is the business instruction done individually? +

The business portion is taught in a group setting. The product-development portion takes place with the instructor connected to the student's chosen pathway.
